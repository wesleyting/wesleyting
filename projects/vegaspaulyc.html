Case Study

VegasPaulyC

Turning a social audience into a global ecommerce business.

Shopify Ecommerce • 2024-2025

Creator commerce, operations, fulfillment, and support systems.

Vegas Pauly C had already built a large audience across TikTok and Instagram, with more than one million followers combined. What he did not have yet was the infrastructure to turn that audience into a business.

There was no website, no ecommerce store, no inventory system, no fulfillment workflow, and no clear operational process behind the scenes.

My role was to help build that foundation, not just by creating the website, but by setting up the systems that allowed his audience to become customers.

Context

From Online Presence to Real Business

When the project started, the brand was mostly Pauly himself: his face, his name, his catchphrases, and the personality his audience already knew from social media.

There was no existing store or formal brand system to build from. The visual direction, merchandise, product setup, and ecommerce experience all had to be created from the ground up.

The goal was not simply to launch a merch store. The goal was to create a digital home for Pauly's brand, while giving fans an easy way to buy products, request experiences, and connect with the team.

My Role

More Than a Website Build

This project quickly became larger than a typical website build.

I worked under Alan Richardson and became the person responsible for bringing the digital and operational side of the business to life. Whenever a new challenge came up, whether it involved the website, product setup, customer support, suppliers, fulfillment, or Shopify, I was usually the person responsible for figuring it out.

A big part of my role was making sure everything was properly set up so the business could keep running without needing constant manual attention.

Design Approach

Designing Around the Creator

Unlike a typical ecommerce store, the homepage could not just be a product grid.

Pauly's audience already knew him through short-form video, so the website needed to feel like an extension of his online presence first. His face, name, personality, and recognizable catchphrases were the brand.

The site had to work as both a personal website and a shop. Visitors needed to immediately understand who Pauly was, then naturally move toward merchandise, fan experiences, and other ways to engage with the brand.

Because most of his audience came from social media, the mobile experience was especially important. The site needed to feel simple, familiar, and easy to use on a phone.

Launch Story

Managing a Preorder Launch Across Continents

One of the more complex parts of the project was launching custom sound buttons.

The product was manufactured in China, shipped to a fulfillment partner in Florida, and then sent to customers across the United States, Canada, the UK, Australia, Switzerland, and other countries.

At the same time, Pauly was already promoting the product to his audience. Fans were asking about the launch date, but the exact arrival timeline was still uncertain because production, international shipping, and warehouse setup all had to line up.

To solve this, I set up a preorder page with a clear estimated timeline, then helped keep the launch organized as the product moved from supplier to warehouse. I coordinated updates, tracked shipping progress, prepared fulfillment before the inventory arrived, and worked with the Florida warehouse so backorders could begin shipping as soon as the product landed.

The result was a smoother launch for customers, with preorder customers receiving their products without complaints.

Systems

Building the System Behind the Store

The website was only one part of the project. Behind it, the business needed systems that could support real customers.

I helped connect the tools needed for ecommerce, fulfillment, customer communication, and product management. Shopify handled the core store experience, while integrations like Printful helped support merchandise production and fulfillment.

I also helped set up contact forms and email channels so fans, customers, and business inquiries had a proper place to go.

The goal was to create a setup that felt simple on the front end, but reliable behind the scenes.

Reflection

What This Project Taught Me

This project taught me that building online is not just about code.

A successful launch depends on design, development, logistics, communication, fulfillment, and customer expectations all working together. My value on this project was being the person trusted to figure things out and keep everything moving.

It was fulfilling because the work became more than a website. It became a real business system that connected a creator, his team, suppliers, designers, fulfillment partners, and fans around the world.
